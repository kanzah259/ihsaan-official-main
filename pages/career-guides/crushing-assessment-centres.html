Crushing Assessment Centres

Prepare for group exercises, interviews, and practical tasks, and show your potential under pressure.

On this page

On this page

Think of it as the final boss battle in the job hunt - a high-stakes stage where employers get to see you in action. Unlike traditional interviews, assessment centres are immersive, multi-part selection processes designed to test how you think, communicate, and collaborate under pressure.

You’re not just being assessed on what you know, but how you operate in real-time. This is done through:

  • Group exercises
  • Case studies or problem-solving tasks
  • One-on-one or panel interviews
  • Presentations (with limited prep time)
  • In-tray or e-tray simulations
  • Role plays or business scenarios
  • Informal networking events (yes — these count, too)

Most assessment centres last anywhere from half a day to two full days, and by the time you’re invited, it means the employer already sees strong potential in you. Now it’s time to show up and seal the deal.

What Employers Are Really Looking For

Spoiler: It’s not perfection. Nobody expects you to be flawless or have all the answers. What they’re actually assessing is:

  • How you think through problems
  • How you behave around others
  • How clearly you communicate your ideas
  • How well you balance leadership with collaboration
  • How you perform under pressure

It’s a test of potential - not just polish. They want to see how you show up when things aren’t scripted.

Prepping Like a Pro

Understand the Company & Role

  • Know their mission, values, current challenges and recent news
  • Understand the role’s responsibilities and what competencies they care about most (refer to the job description)
  • Learn their competitors, market, and tone - culture fit does matter, especially at competitive firms

Reflect & Rehearse

Use the STAR method (Situation, Task, Action, Result) to prepare strong stories around:

  • Leading a team
  • Handling failure
  • Solving problems
  • Resolving conflict
  • Influencing others
  • Working under time pressure

Practice Typical Tasks

Prep platforms like AssessmentDay, JobTestPrep, or YouTube videos can help you simulate:

  • Group discussions
  • Case studies
  • Psychometric or aptitude tests
  • Time-bound presentations
  • In-tray/e-tray scenarios

Also: sleep well, eat well, stay hydrated. Basic, yes - but crucial for mental performance and sharpness.

Group Exercises: Play the Social Game Strategically

Group tasks are there to test collaboration, communication, and leadership — not dominance.

How to shine:

  • Actively listen and build on others’ ideas
  • Encourage quieter voices: “Sarah, what’s your take on this?”
  • Offer to structure the task or keep time: “Let’s keep track so we stay focused.”
  • Summarise what’s been discussed to move things forward: “So far, it sounds like we’re leaning toward...”

What to avoid:

  • Talking over others or dominating the task
  • Sticking to just one role (e.g., only being the timekeeper)
  • Getting flustered when the task gets messy — that’s part of the challenge

Pro tip: Be the person who helps the group move — not just speak.

Interviews: Behavioural & Strength-Based

You’ll likely face one (or more) interviews. These could be competency-based, strength-based, or a mix of both.

Competency Questions:

Prepare those STAR stories!

  • “Tell me about a time you handled a difficult team member.”
  • “Describe a time you worked under pressure.”
  • “Give an example of when you led a project successfully.”

Strength-Based Questions:

Focus on energy, not just experience.

  • “What comes naturally to you?”
  • “What tasks energise you the most?”
  • “When do you feel most productive?”

Be honest. These are designed to uncover your fit and what motivates you.

Case Studies & Presentations: Thinking on Your Feet

You’ll be given a business scenario and a short window to:

  • Analyse the info
  • Form a recommendation
  • Present your solution (often solo, sometimes in a group)

How to approach it:

  1. Scan quickly for key numbers, trends, or goals
  2. Prioritise issues - don’t try to fix everything
  3. Structure clearly:
    • Intro: What’s the problem?
    • Main points: What matters most?
    • Recommendation: What’s your call?

Speak confidently - even if you’re unsure. You’re being assessed on how you structure and communicate, not just the final answer.

In-Tray & E-Tray: Simulate the Job

You’ll handle realistic emails, reports, and memos - prioritising tasks, responding to requests, or flagging issues.

What to show:

  • Time management and decision-making
  • Logic in prioritisation (urgency vs. importance)
  • Professional written tone
  • Clarity in reasoning

Tip: Imagine yourself already in the role. What would your manager expect?

Informal Events: Still Under the Microscope

Chatting with candidates or recruiters over lunch? Small talk during breaks?

These moments are still part of the assessment. They give insights into your professionalism, curiosity, and interpersonal skills.

  • Ask thoughtful questions
  • Show genuine interest in others
  • Be polite, relaxed, and present

Staying Calm is the Secret Weapon

Assessment centres are designed to test your limits, but they’re not meant to break you. If something doesn’t go perfectly, let it go and move on - there are multiple chances to impress.

  • Focus on each task, one at a time
  • Be flexible and self-aware
  • Don’t fake it - show up as a switched-on version of yourself
  • Remember: they’re hiring for potential, not perfection
