Gaining Industry Experience

Explore internships, Spring Weeks, and Insight Days, find opportunities, and prepare stronger applications.

On this page

On this page

One of the best ways to boost your employability and secure a graduate role is through early exposure to the working world. Whether it's a Spring Week, Insight Day, or Internship, these experiences help you build key skills, understand industries, and grow your network.

In many cases, doing well during an internship can lead to a return offer-a fast-track to a full-time graduate job.

Key Terms Explained

Internships

Short-term, paid work experiences (usually over summer) normally for students in their penultimate year or recent graduates. Internships help you apply what you’ve learned, explore career paths, and gain valuable contacts.

Spring Weeks

1-2 week placements in March or April designed for first-year students (or second years on a four-year course). These give early insight into sectors like finance, consulting, law, and tech usually being provided exclusively by major firms.

Insight Days

Single-day or short events for students of any year (sometimes even sixth formers). They involve talks, networking, and workshops to help you explore different industries and company cultures.

Why It Matters: Zain vs Hasan

Imagine Zain and Hasan both apply for a graduate software engineering role. Both have great grades and similar backgrounds.

But Hasan completed an internship last summer. Zain didn’t.

All else being equal, Hasan is more likely to get the job-because he has relevant industry experience. Employers value proven interest and practical skills.

Tips for Application Questions

If applications involve detailed questions (e.g. motivation, strengths), use the STAR method (we go into more detail in the interviews page):

Situation What was the context?

Task – What were you asked to do?

Action – What did you personally do?

Result – What was the outcome? Be specific and quantify if you can!

FAQ

Internships

When do internship applications open?

Most open from August to October for internships the following summer. Check early and apply ASAP since this can vary greatly. In general the larger the company the earlier they conduct the application process.

Do I need a Spring Week to get an internship?

No, but it helps. Many students secure internships without Spring Weeks by demonstrating relevant skills through other experiences (e.g. student societies, projects, research, part-time jobs).

Can I apply for internships in my first year?

Some industries (e.g. tech, startups, NGOs) offer first-year internships, but they are rarer. Most formal internships are aimed at penultimate-year students. Have a look at Spring Weeks and Insight days in addition.

Can an Internship lead into a graduate job?

Yes! Many firms use internships as pipelines for graduate recruitment. A return offer saves them time and effort later.

Spring Weeks

Who can apply for Spring Weeks?

Spring Weeks are usually designed for first-year students (or second-year students on a four-year course). They help you explore industries like finance, consulting, and law early on.

Can a Spring Week lead to an internship?

Definitely. Perform well, and you may be fast-tracked into a summer internship.

When should I apply for Spring Weeks?

Applications open in September–October and often close by December–January. Early bird = better chances!

How competitive are Spring Weeks?

Very competitive, especially in finance and consulting. A strong application with good academics, extracurriculars, and a well-prepared cover letter will help.

What if I don’t get a Spring Week?

No stress. You can still build experience through uni societies, side projects, or volunteering. It’s all valuable! Make sure to not dwell on your losses and keep trying
