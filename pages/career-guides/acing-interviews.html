Acing Interviews

Prepare for different interview formats, make a strong first impression, and convey your skills and motivation with confidence.

So you’re at the interview stage, well done! That means the company already sees potential in you to some extent. You just need to convey your passion, aptitude and capabilities for the job during the interview.

Understanding the Interview Landscape

It important to know what type of interview you are facing and what to expect. You can do this by:

  • Checking the job description (sometimes it’s listed)
  • Checking the interview confirmation email
  • Emailing HR or the recruiter

Interviews come in different forms, here are a couple:

  • Behavioural: To assess how you handled real-life situations in the past. (Tell me about a time...)
  • Technical: To test your role-specific knowledge or hands-on ability. (Coding tests, portfolio reviews)
  • Situational: To assess your approach to hypothetical work scenarios. (What would you do if...)
  • Case Studies / Estimation: To assess your business and strategy toolkit. (Most common in consulting)
  • Group / Panel Interviews: To observe how you interact in team settings or with multiple decision-makers.

Preparation

Research the Company

Look into:

  • The company’s mission & values
  • Their workplace culture (LinkedIn posts, Glassdoor, team bios)
  • Recent news, projects, or updates to reference in your conversation
  • Your interviewer (normally they tell you who this is in your invite email)

Practice Common Questions

  • For behavioural questions have stories and anecdotes from your experiences to talk about in different situations. Use the STAR(R) Method:
    • Situation – Set the scene
      • “In my previous digital marketing job, the company wanted to get more people to sign up to a newsletter which was not receiving a lot of attention.”
    • Task – Explain what you needed to achieve
      • “my job was to find a way of getting more people to sign up.”
    • Action – Describe what you did
      • “I organised a meeting with other members of the marketing team to come up with creative ideas, and I led the social media campaign to generate interest in the revamped newsletter.”
    • Result – Share the outcome (the most important bit!), try to make it quantifiable
      • “over a period of 3 months, there was an 25% increase in sign-ups to the newsletter and the approach I took was used by the management team in other departments.”
    • Reflection – (Optional) Reflect on what you learned or would do differently
  • Have answers ready to common general questions like:
    • “Tell me about yourself”
    • “What’s your biggest strength?”
    • “How do you handle feedback?”

Making a Solid First Impression

The first ten seconds of meeting your interviewer a critical deciding factor for whether you will be hired or not.

Remote Interviews (Zoom, Google Meet, etc.)

Setup

  • Find a quiet, well-lit space with a plain background
  • Use headphones or earbuds for clear audio
  • Sit at a desk or table, not on your bed
  • Elevate your laptop to eye level (books work!)

Camera and Body Language

  • Keep your camera on unless otherwise requested
  • Sit up straight, smile, and look into the webcam (not the screen)
  • Use your hands naturally when speaking - even virtually, it shows energy!

In-Person Interviews

Logistics Are Key

  • Plan your route and leave buffer time (plan to be 15 mins early)
  • Bring a copy of your CV/resume, a notebook, and a pen
  • Greet the receptionist and everyone else you interact with professionally - they may give feedback

Presence & Poise

  • Greet with confidence (smile, handshake or polite nod)
  • Make good eye contact (as culturally appropriate)
  • Pay attention to posture and body language

How do I manage handshakes with the opposite gender?

For many Muslim students, this is a common concern - but it can be handled professionally and respectfully, without awkwardness or discomfort.

The Best Approach: Communicate in Advance

If you're attending an in-person interview, and the company offers a chance to request any “reasonable adjustments,” take that opportunity to mention your handshake preference due to faith.

Successful Real Life Example at Tier 1 Engineering Firm:

A Muslim student used this exact approach when replying to a Tier 1 engineering company. The result? Interviewers were informed ahead of time and simply didn’t offer a handshake - no awkwardness, no explanations needed, and he got the job!

Exact Wording for Email Used:

“Thank you for your email. I confirm that I have received the interview details, job description, and Graduate Interview FAQ sheet. I appreciate the resources provided and will review them accordingly. I formally accept the invite to the interview.

Due to faith-based reasons, I may refrain from physical contact, including handshakes. I wanted to mention this in advance to ensure a comfortable and respectful interaction. I’d be grateful if this could be communicated to the interview panel.

Thank you very much again for your support. Please let me know if you need anything else from my side.”

If a Handshake Is Offered

If it still happens on the spot, don’t panic. Simply:

  • Smile warmly
  • Place your hand over your heart
  • Say something like:

    “I don’t shake hands for faith-based reasons - I hope that’s okay!”

Setting this boundary early on is a great example of being principled, polite, and professional - and it's fully possible to do while earning the respect of others and the pleasure of Allah.

Asking good questions

Asking smart, well-timed questions isn’t just a formality -it's a powerful way to show curiosity, preparation, and genuine interest in the role and the company.

When you ask good questions, you’re telling the interviewer:

“I care about doing well here, and I’m thinking long-term.”

You can ask questions about:

  • The Role: “What does a typical day in this role look like?”
  • The Company/Team: “I saw your team recently launched [project or initiative] - how was that experience?”
  • Development: “What does progression typically look like in this role or team?”
  • Next Steps: “What are the next stages in the interview process?”

Remember to not ask questions that have already been answered since it may show that you weren't listening.

Bonus Tip: Current Events

At Fatima's very first final-round interview, she thought she was ready. She had practiced all the classic questions...

But then, the interviewer smiled and threw in:

"Can you discuss a recent news event and its impact on your role?"

Fatima froze. No one had prepared her for this.

Later that evening, as she reflected, she realised - this question wasn't just about news. It was about showing the interviewer you understand your industry, you keep up to date, and you can think critically.

Since then, she never skipped preparing for this question. Here’s how you can avoid the same mistake.

The Simple Formula (NEWS Framework)

Fatima learned this framework to structure her answer:

  • N – News Event: What happened?
  • E – Effect: Why does it matter for the industry?
  • W – Work Role: Why does it matter to the job you want?
  • S – Strategy: What would you do about it?
